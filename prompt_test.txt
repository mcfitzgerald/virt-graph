  You are a supply chain analyst with access to a virtual twin. The twin consists of:

  1. An ontology at pcg_example/ontology/pcg.yaml — read this first. It describes 38 entity classes (TBox) and 50 relationships (RBox) for a
  consumer packaged goods supply chain. Pay attention to the vg: annotations — they tell you the table names, primary keys, foreign keys,
  operation types, state machines, flow configs, and domain semantics.
  2. A DuckDB database at pcg_example/data/demo.duckdb — a curated ~2000-row dataset across all 38 tables. Connect with
  duckdb.connect('pcg_example/data/demo.duckdb', read_only=True).
  3. The DDL at erp_schema_duckdb.sql — read this if you need column names or types beyond what the ontology declares.

  Your workflow for every question:
  1. Read the ontology to identify which classes and relationships are relevant
  2. Use the vg:context blocks for business logic and query hints
  3. Generate SQL and execute it against the DuckDB
  4. Analyze the results and explain what the data means in business terms

  Important data conventions (from the ontology's context blocks):
  - order_lines.unit_price is ALWAYS zero — revenue lives in ar_invoice_lines
  - orders.source_id maps to channels.id (1=CLUB, 2=GROCERY, 3=CONVENIENCE)
  - formula_ingredients.ingredient_id is polymorphic — points to ingredients (1-15) or bulk_intermediates (51-56)
  - route_segments uses origin_type/destination_type with values: supplier, plant, rdc, customer_dc, store
  - Shipments come in 3-leg chains: plant_to_rdc → rdc_to_customer_dc → customer_dc_to_store
  - ar_invoices.channel directly gives you the channel name (CLUB/GROCERY/CONVENIENCE)

  The Questions

  Start simple, build toward the C2D story, then push into kinetic territory.

  Layer 1 — Orientation (prove the ontology works as context)

  1. How many tables are in this supply chain and how are they organized by domain?
  2. What are the three graph patterns declared in the ontology and what operations does each support?
  3. Which relationships cross domain boundaries? What does that tell us about where visibility breaks down?

  Layer 2 — Visibility (the C2D waterfall)

  4. What is the margin on SKU 1 (Premium Whitening Toothpaste) by channel? Decompose into revenue per case, COGS, and freight per case.
  5. Why is Convenience freight so much higher than Club? Break it down by shipment leg.
  6. Within Convenience, how does last-mile freight vary by store location?
  7. Which product category has the lowest margin through Grocery? Is any category margin-negative?

  Layer 3 — Cross-Domain Tracing (connecting the silos)

  8. Trace the full cost stack for a single Convenience order to a New York store — from PO to AR receipt. What accrues at each stage?
  9. The HDPE bottle supplier (AmeriPack) is overcharging. How much? What's the total open variance?
  10. How many Citrus Premix batches have yield below 90%? What's the material cost of that waste?

  Layer 4 — State & Flow (kinetic)

  11. What's the DSO (days sales outstanding) by channel? What's the working capital implication?
  12. Show me the state distribution for all entity types that have state machines declared in the ontology.
  13. Trace the Order-to-Cash flow for order 5001 — every entity it touches, in sequence, with the dollar amount at each stage.

  Layer 5 — What-If (simulation)

  14. If we fix Citrus Premix yield from 83% to 97%, how much material cost do we save annually (extrapolate from the 90-day window)?
  15. If we drop the two highest-freight Convenience stores, what happens to average Convenience margin?
  16. If we renegotiate AmeriPack HDPE bottles back to $0.68, what's the annual savings?
